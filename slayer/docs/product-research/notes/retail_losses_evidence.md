# How and Why US Retail Options Traders Lose Money (incl. 0DTE): Academic and Regulatory Evidence

Researcher notes, compiled 2026-11-07. US market, 2020-2026 evidence prioritised. Key source PDFs were read in full text where noted ("full text read"); other items are from abstracts or secondary summaries and are marked as such.

## Q1. What do the main studies find, and what share of losses comes from spreads/execution vs. timing vs. overpaying for volatility vs. lottery preferences?

### Takeaway
Every major study finds retail option *buyers* lose money on average, and the largest identifiable part of the loss is execution cost (the bid-ask spread / effective half-spread paid to wholesalers and market makers), not bad direction calls. Buying short-dated, cheap options carries the most cost. Paying too much for implied volatility before events (earnings) is a second, separate cause. Retail *sellers* are on average profitable. Evidence for "lottery-seeking" as a loss driver is mixed: retail buys lottery-like contracts, but trader-level data do not show lottery-like realized payoffs.

### Cited Findings

**Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya, "Retail Trading in Options and the Rise of the Big Three Wholesalers," Journal of Finance 78(6), 2023, pp. 3465-3514 (full text read)**
- Sample: Nov 4, 2019 - Jun 30, 2021, built from OPRA trades flagged as single-leg price-improvement-mechanism trades (wholesaler internalisation), used as a proxy for retail. Retail trading "recently reached over 60% of total market volume" in options. Nearly 90% of options PFOF comes from three wholesalers (Citadel, Susquehanna, Wolverine) — [JF via LBS repository](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- Retail prefers cheaper weekly options with an average quoted bid-ask spread of 12.6%. 50% of retail trades are in options with under one week to expiry. A subset of these carries average quoted spreads of 29% — [same](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- Assuming a 10-day holding period, retail lost **$2.1 billion** on options from Nov 2019 to Jun 2021. Losses are negative at every horizon tested (1, 2, 5 and 10 days, and to expiry) — [same](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- "The bulk of the losses comes from the indirect costs of trading." Aggregate trading costs (trade price vs. midquote, i.e. effective half-spread × size) were **$6.4 billion**. That is far more than direct commission costs (~$900 million) and more than total options PFOF over the sample (~$2.8 billion). The authors note these costs "are not as transparent as brokerage fees and are likely to be overlooked by retail investors" — [same](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- Losses are concentrated in short-term contracts under every performance measure. The data show two groups: buyers of short-term options, who lose, and sellers, who "make significant profits, even after transaction costs" — [same](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- PFOF economics: in 2021, US brokers received $2.4 billion in PFOF for options vs. $1.3 billion for equities. Options PFOF exceeded equity PFOF by about 100% in every month of the sample, even though only a fraction of retail investors trade options — [same](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- The retail measure tracks attention: it rises with WallStreetBets mentions in meme-stock frenzies and drops during retail brokerage outages — [same](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- One secondary summary says that "after trading costs retail trades lost more than $4 billion" in the BPS sample. That conflicts with the paper's $2.1B headline. The $4.5B appears in the paper as an alternative-measure figure ("−$2.2 billion (−$4.5 billion)") — [Evidence Investor summary](https://www.evidenceinvestor.com/post/how-profitable-is-retail-options-trading). Treat $2.1B (10-day horizon) as the headline number.

**de Silva, Smith & So, "Losing is Optional: Retail Option Trading and Expected Announcement Volatility" (MIT Sloan working paper, 2022-2023; abstract and summaries only)**
- Retail investors buy options before earnings announcements, especially where expected volatility is high. They "overpay for options relative to realized volatility, incur enormous bid-ask spreads, and do not close their positions until weeks after announcements." Average losses are about 5-9%, and 10-14% for high expected-volatility announcements — [AFA listing](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=122472); [Stanford GSB listing](https://gsbpreserve.stanford.edu/view/63824/losing-is-optional-retail-option-trading-and-expected-announcement-volatility); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/how-retail-losses-money-in-option-trading/)
- Average bid-ask spreads paid are about 20% of option price, and wider before high-uncertainty announcements. Slow exits after IV crush are not explained by rational hedging (secondary summary) — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/how-retail-losses-money-in-option-trading/); [tastylive coverage](https://tastylive.com/news-insights/new-research-says-retail-options-traders-keep-making-this-same-mistake)
- Related paper: Lipson, Tomio & Zhang (2023) find stocks with larger increases in retail options trading saw larger increases in volatility — [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-options-trading-is-increasing-the-volatility-of-securities)

**Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda, "Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options... But Should They?" (SSRN 4404704, 2023; summaries)**
- Data: Cboe SPX option trades with trader-type codes, Jan 2021 - Feb 2023. Average retail 0DTE losses were $184k per day over the full sample and $358k per day after daily expirations began (May 2022). Total was about $70 million, of which more than $50 million was transaction costs — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-04-21/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=44172); [Neudata review](https://neudata.co/literature-reviews/zero-day-to-expiry-options-a-losing-bet-for-retail-traders)
- About 60% of daily losses came from transaction costs (and separately, about 60% from 0DTE puts; the two overlap). Retail *selling* decisions were profitable, while buying carried most of the losses. Retail trades roughly aligned with how expensive 0DTEs were, so timing was not the main problem; costs were. The authors call it "a cautionary tale against allowing unrestricted access for retail investors to 0DTE options." About 75% of retail SPX option trades were 0DTE, and the retail share of 0DTE volume rose above 6% in 2022 (from about 4%) — [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=44172)

**Bogousslavsky & Muravyev, "An Anatomy of Retail Option Trading" (working paper, version Aug 29, 2024; full text read)**
- Trader-level data from a trading-journal provider whose users link their broker accounts so trades are imported and verified. Covers 5,182 traders, 2.4M parent trades (0.9M option trades), about $15B, 2020-2022. The option share of trades rose from 23% (Jan 2020) to 49% (Dec 2022). 26% of option trades are on the S&P 500 index. Median option maturity fell from 4 days (2020) to 1 day (2022). 0DTE was 23% of option trades, and 68.8% of option trades were day trades — [LSU-hosted PDF](https://www.lsu.edu/business/files/event-files/2025-finance-mardi-gras/retail_option_trading_v2.pdf)
- The average option trade returned **−0.9%**, which is "small compared to typical option bid-ask spreads of 5% to 10%" and comparable to fees. The authors suggest these traders use limit orders to avoid paying the full spread. Option purchases lost 3.95% on average. Naked option sales earned about 20% (about +24-28% incremental) — [same](https://www.lsu.edu/business/files/event-files/2025-finance-mardi-gras/retail_option_trading_v2.pdf)
- **0DTE trades lose 4.6% per trade, while other trades earn about 0%.** 93.3% of 0DTE trades open with a purchase. The 0DTE coefficient is −4.7% univariate and −2.95% with controls. Index/ETF option trades returned −3%, vs. +0.15% for single-stock options. Small traders lost the most (−3.4% per option trade) and multi-day traders lost about 0.9% — [same](https://www.lsu.edu/business/files/event-files/2025-finance-mardi-gras/retail_option_trading_v2.pdf)
- Lottery preference: there is "little evidence" of positive skewness in realized dollar profits. For option purchases the 10th/90th percentile P&L was −$296 / +$217. Traders realise gains earlier than losses (disposition effect), which adds negative skew. The main motive identified is affordability: traders use options to get exposure to high-priced underlyings, and after a stock split, propensity to trade the stock rather than its options rises about 10% — [same](https://www.lsu.edu/business/files/event-files/2025-finance-mardi-gras/retail_option_trading_v2.pdf)
- Caveat: the authors say their sample is "relatively sophisticated" (journal users, large trades, more shorting). A CXO summary of a later version cites ~$20B and −0.8% per trade, so figures differ slightly across versions — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/actual-retail-option-trading-returns)

**Cboe's own data on 0DTE retail (industry, not peer reviewed)**
- Cboe "0DTEs Decoded" (May 2025, Mandy Xu): about 92-95% of SPX 0DTE volume is in defined-risk strategies. More than half of retail opening trades have max loss under $2,000. Retail favours iron condors and butterflies. Unhedged naked shorts are about 4% of activity. Estimates of the retail share of 0DTE volume range from 30-40% (older Cboe) to 50-60% (newer) — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact); [FOW](https://www.fow.com/insights/analysis-cboe-points-to-retail-flow-as-zero-day-options-grow)
- Cboe (2023) said it does not see customers trading 0DTE "predominantly for speculative purposes" and that flows are balanced — [Cboe Volatility Insights](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)
- I found **no Cboe-published measure of retail 0DTE P&L**. Cboe's data describe risk shape and strategy mix, not profitability.
- 0DTE was reportedly 59% of SPX option volume in 2025 (secondary source citing Cboe; not verified at primary) — [WilmerHale / FINRA filing context](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260423-sec-approves-amendments-to-finra-rule-4210-replacing-day-trading-margin-requirements-with-a-modernized-intraday-margin-standard)

**Dim, Eraker & Vilkov, "0DTEs: Trading, Gamma Risk and Volatility Propagation" (SSRN 4692190, 2024; summaries)**
- Market-stability paper, not a retail-P&L paper. They find no evidence that 0DTE open-interest gamma unconditionally raises index volatility. Higher market-maker gamma is linked to *lower* intraday realised volatility. 0DTE and underlying markets have grown more integrated over time — [Harbourfront Quant summary](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/the-rise-of-0dte-options-cause-for); [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)

**Bandi, Fusari & Renò, "0DTE Option Pricing" (working paper 2023-2025; summaries)**
- Proposes a pricing model for the 0DTE implied-vol surface and estimates 0DTE variance and return risk premia. A JHU summary frames this as risk/reward insight for 0DTE buyers vs. sellers — [JHU Carey](https://carey.jhu.edu/news/risk-reward-insights-0dte-option-trading); [ESSEC listing](https://faculty.essec.edu/en/research/15649-0dte-option-pricing). I could not open the full text, so no numeric premia are reported here.

**Other 2024-2026 papers**
- "Do Retail Traders Gamble on Stock Options?" (Journal of Financial Markets, 2026): finds retail options trading correlates with WallStreetBets mentions and Robinhood ownership breadth, which points to speculative rather than hedging motives — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418126000170); [FMA draft](https://www.fmaconferences.org/Taipei/Papers/6.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Decomposition (my calculation from BPS, not stated by the authors):** BPS net 10-day loss was $2.1B and effective spread cost was $6.4B. That implies retail trades valued at the midquote would have been roughly break-even or positive over the sample. If so, **essentially all of the aggregate loss is execution cost**, not stock-picking or timing. Beckmeyer et al. agree for 0DTE: more than 60-70% of losses are transaction costs ($50M+ of $70M). Bogousslavsky-Muravyev find small losses for traders who probably use limit orders, which also points to spread capture as the decisive variable.
- **Overpaying for volatility** is a separate, event-specific driver (de Silva et al.: 5-14% losses around earnings, made worse by holding through IV crush). It is not a separate number in the aggregate studies, so no study gives a clean share split across "spread vs. timing vs. IV overpay vs. lottery."
- **Lottery preference** explains *what* retail buys (cheap, short-dated, OTM, high-spread contracts), and that choice drives the cost. Trader-level data do not show lottery-like realised payoffs. Lottery preference therefore works mostly by pushing traders into the highest-cost contracts.
- Losses are asymmetric: buyers lose and sellers win, consistently across BPS, Beckmeyer and Bogousslavsky-Muravyev.
- A transparency tool could target: (1) effective spread paid vs. mid, per trade and cumulative; (2) IV paid vs. subsequent realised move (especially for earnings and 0DTE); (3) days held after the event / IV crush; (4) P&L split by buy vs. sell and by DTE bucket.

### Gaps
- No study I found cleanly apportions losses across spread, timing, IV premium and lottery preference in one framework.
- No Cboe or OCC dataset on retail 0DTE *profitability*; Cboe's figures cover risk shape only.
- No 2024-2026 peer-reviewed update of Beckmeyer et al. with post-2023 0DTE data (single-stock 0DTE, daily expiries on QQQ/SPY and others).
- de Silva et al. publication status and final journal numbers not verified (working-paper numbers used).
- Bandi/Fusari/Renò variance-risk-premium estimates not obtained (full text not accessible).

## Q2. Payment for order flow, price improvement and fill quality in options: what is the evidence, and can a retail trader verify their own fill quality?

### Takeaway
Options PFOF is larger per dollar than equity PFOF and highly concentrated. Academic evidence links PFOF-paying market makers to less price improvement for retail options orders. Unlike equities, options have no SEC Rule 605 execution-quality statistics. Rule 606 discloses routing and PFOF but not fill quality. In practice a retail trader cannot verify their options fill quality from mandated disclosures; they would have to compare their fills with NBBO/midquote data themselves.

### Cited Findings
- Ernst & Spatt (NBER w29883): using variation in designated market maker (DMM) assignments, retail options traders get less price improvement and worse prices from market makers that pay PFOF to brokers — [NBER w29883](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29883/w29883.pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4068065)
- Ernst & Spatt, "Payment for Order Flow and Option Internalization," Review of Financial Studies 2026 (hhaf108). Option internalisation is imperfectly competitive. An average retail option trade earns the broker about 40¢ per 100 shares equivalent vs. 20¢ for stock. Per dollar invested, options can generate about 10× the broker revenue of equities, which creates pressure to steer clients toward more frequent and complex options trading. DMMs receive the first five contracts of orders they bring. Citadel and Susquehanna together hold 60% of DMM positions and buy over 70% of retail option flow. Exchange fee rules discourage outside bidders in price-improvement auctions. Recommended policy: more options data disclosure and lower certain exchange fees — [CMU Tepper release, Mar 2026](https://www.cmu.edu/tepper/news/stories/high-option-trading-fees-barriers-competition); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4056512)
- One literature review cites PFOF at about 8 bps for options vs. 0.8 bps for equities (Ernst & Spatt 2022) (secondary) — [search summary of literature; primary is NBER w29883](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29883/w29883.pdf)
- BPS: wholesalers execute retail options through paired price-improvement auctions where "the fees set by exchanges are stacked against responders, and it is prohibitively expensive to break up many of these paired trades." The SEC's PFOF focus "has focused almost exclusively on equities." BPS note Gensler once cited options as an example of *superior* retail execution, a view their spread data contradict — [JF 2023](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- SEC DERA working paper "How Does Payment for Order Flow Influence Markets?" (Jan 2025) exists as SEC staff research on PFOF (not read in full) — [SEC DERA](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera_wp_payment-order-flow-2501.pdf)
- **Rule 605** (execution-quality statistics) amendments adopted Mar 6, 2024 cover NMS stocks only. The proposal "contain[ed] no mention of options." Industry had asked for options coverage since 2008/2014 — [Seward & Kissel](https://sewkis.com/?p=14103); [S3](https://s3.com/s3-tracking-best-execution/); [Markets Media](https://www.marketsmedia.com/routing-disclosures-options-sought)
- **Rule 606** covers both NMS stocks and listed options. The 2018 amendments added more detailed routing and PFOF disclosures, and brokers must tell a customer on request where their orders were routed. It reports routing and payments, not execution quality — [TradePMR order routing disclosures](https://www.tradepmr.com/order-routing-disclosures); [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-legal-compliance/sec-requires-broker-dealers-to-disclose-more-about-client-orders/76825)
- The SEC's Dec 2022 package (Reg Best Execution, which did reach options, and the equity-only Order Competition Rule) addressed options only through Best Ex — [Seward & Kissel](https://sewkis.com/?p=14103)

### Inferences
- The disclosure gap is clear. Equities get standardised fill-quality statistics (Rule 605). Options get only routing and PFOF disclosure (Rule 606), which says how much the broker is paid, not what the customer received. BPS say the spread cost of $6.4B is "likely to be overlooked by retail investors." Both points support a **per-trade fill-quality ledger** (fill vs. NBBO mid at time of fill, price improvement in $ and %, cumulative "spread tax") as a high-value, currently missing transparency feature.
- Because options trade only on exchanges and OPRA publishes NBBO, a third-party tool with timestamped fills and historical NBBO can compute effective spread per fill. The main obstacle is data cost and timestamp precision, not regulation.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether the SEC's Dec 2022 Reg Best Ex and Order Competition proposals were formally withdrawn in 2025. Check the SEC's withdrawal notices before relying on this.
- No published dataset of options price improvement by broker comparable to equity 605 data was found.
- No study measures how many retail options traders check or understand Rule 606 reports.

## Q3. What do regulators say (FINRA/SEC alerts on 0DTE and options, 2024-2026 rulemaking, enforcement against signal/alert services and finfluencers)?

### Takeaway
FINRA has a 0DTE investor-education piece (dated June 2026), and the largest related rule change is the SEC-approved replacement of the $25k pattern-day-trader rule with intraday margin standards (Apr 2026). Its stated aims include stopping unmargined expiration-day options positions from building up. Enforcement against alert sellers has come mainly from the FTC (deceptive earnings claims) and the SEC/DOJ (finfluencer pump-and-dumps), with FINRA fining firms for poor supervision of finfluencers. I found no SEC case targeting an *options-alert* service specifically.

### Cited Findings
- FINRA Investor Insights, "Zeroing In on an Options Trading Strategy: 0DTE" (listed June 4, 2026). It warns that buying and selling 0DTEs can be risky, gives worked examples (e.g., a short uncovered call collecting $1,000 but losing $4,000), and notes that intraday margin applies to options on their expiration date — [FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/zeroing-in-options-trading-strategy); [FINRA What's New](https://www.finra.org/investors/whats-new)
- FINRA Regulatory Notice 22-08 (2022) sought comment on stricter regulation of complex products and options for retail. Bogousslavsky & Muravyev cite it as a response to retail options concerns — [Bogousslavsky & Muravyev PDF](https://www.lsu.edu/business/files/event-files/2025-finance-mardi-gras/retail_option_trading_v2.pdf)
- SR-FINRA-2025-017: on Apr 14, 2026 the SEC approved replacing FINRA Rule 4210 day-trading margin requirements, including the $25,000 PDT minimum, with intraday margin standards based on actual intraday exposure (Regulatory Notice 26-10, Apr 20, 2026). FINRA's filing says requiring margin for options on their expiration dates is meant to prevent unmargined positions building up. Schwab reports an effective date of June 4, 2026, with firms having until Oct 20, 2027 to implement — [FINRA weekly update](https://www.finra.org/compliance-tools/weekly-archive/04152026); [SEC approval order](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-105226.pdf); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260423-sec-approves-amendments-to-finra-rule-4210-replacing-day-trading-margin-requirements-with-a-modernized-intraday-margin-standard); [Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/sec-approves-scrapping-25000-day-trader-minimum)
- SEC/DOJ, Dec 2022: eight social-media influencers charged over an alleged ~$100M pump-and-dump. They allegedly portrayed themselves as successful traders on Twitter and Discord, touted picks and dumped into the rise (allegations) — [Vinson & Elkins](https://velaw.com/insights/sec-charges-eight-social-media-influencers-takeaways-for-companies-and-individuals); [InvestmentNews](https://investmentnews.com/fintech/social-media-finfluencers-face-charges-in-114-million-pump-and-dump-scheme/230293)
- FINRA's finfluencer sweep led to firm fines for failing to supervise finfluencer content, e.g., M1 Finance ($850k) and TradeZero America ($250k) (secondary) — [Securities Lawyer 101](https://www.securitieslawyer101.com/2025/regulation-of-financial-influencers/)
- A former registered rep turned finfluencer ("K Money") was sentenced to two years for investment-adviser fraud (2026) — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-legal-compliance/ex-indy-rep-turned-phony-finfluencer-gets-two-years-in-prison/267800)
- FTC v. Raging Bull (filed Dec 2020): a stock and options trading-alert / "guru" service with deceptive earnings claims (followers could "double or triple" accounts, results not typical). Settlement reported at $2.4M, with an earnings-claims ban and easier cancellation — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/antitrust/investment-site-to-pay-ftc-2-4-million-for-earnings-deception); [FKKS advertising law](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102hlo0/raging-mad-subscription-program-provokes-ire-of-consumers-and-the-ftc). A blog's "$137M" figure is unverified.
- FTC v. Warrior Trading (2022): $3M settlement over false moneymaking claims for a day-trading education and alerts business. Refunds paid out in 2024 — [FTC business blog](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2022/04/ftc-bids-good-night-deceptive-day-trading-earnings-claims); [FTC consumer alert](https://www.consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2022/03/investment-trading-operation-will-pay-millions-empty-promises)
- Massachusetts v. Robinhood (Jan 2024): $7.5M fine. Robinhood agreed to stop celebratory imagery tied to trading frequency and game-of-chance features (confetti, push notifications, top-movers lists) for MA customers. The state alleged about 68% of MA Robinhood customers approved for options reported limited or no investment experience. The order makes no finding that these features changed behaviour — [Vinson & Elkins](https://velaw.com/insights/game-over-robinhood-pays-7-5-million-to-resolve-gamification-securities-violations); [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-legal-compliance/robinhood-to-pay-75m-to-settle-massachusetts-charges/248214)

### Inferences
- The legal risk for alert and signal businesses centres on **unsubstantiated or atypical performance claims** (FTC Act) and **undisclosed self-dealing / scalping** (SEC anti-fraud). A tool that publishes audited, complete track records (including losers and execution-adjusted returns) addresses exactly what enforcement targets.
- Ending the $25k PDT rule (implementation through Oct 2027) will likely let more small accounts day-trade options. Since small traders lose most per trade (−3.4% in Bogousslavsky-Muravyev), demand for cost and risk transparency may grow.

### Gaps
- No SEC or FINRA enforcement action specifically against an *options flow / unusual activity alert* service was found.
- No SEC investor bulletin specific to 0DTE was found (FINRA's is the main one).
- Status of the SEC's 2023 predictive-data-analytics / digital-engagement proposal (reportedly withdrawn 2025) not verified here.

## Q4. How do retail traders behave around unusual-flow alerts, social media and gamification? Is following flow alerts profitable?

### Takeaway
Retail options activity clearly follows attention (WallStreetBets mentions, Robinhood popularity, meme frenzies), and gamification raises risk-taking in experiments. I found **no academic study testing whether following retail "unusual options activity" or flow-alert products is profitable**. The academic informed-trading literature shows some options volume predicts returns, but that research uses data and signals that differ from public retail alert feeds, and warns that uninformed flow is easily mistaken for informed flow.

### Cited Findings
- BPS's retail options measure co-moves with WallStreetBets mentions during meme-stock frenzies and drops during brokerage outages — [JF 2023](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- Retail options trading correlates with WSB ticker mentions and Robinhood ownership breadth, indicating speculative motives — [J. Financial Markets 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418126000170)
- Chapkovski, Khapko & Zoican (2021, experiment) find gamification induces risk-taking among novice traders and makes their trading noisier. Kalda et al. (2021) find smartphone trading leads investors to buy riskier, lottery-type assets (both as cited in BPS) — [JF 2023](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- In Bogousslavsky-Muravyev's journal data, 76% of accounts follow nobody and 87% have no followers. Social copying within that platform is minimal — [PDF](https://www.lsu.edu/business/files/event-files/2025-finance-mardi-gras/retail_option_trading_v2.pdf)
- Informed-trading literature: Augustin, Brenner, Grass & Subrahmanyam find unusual pre-announcement options activity, and call (put) volume with high expected returns predicts positive (negative) corporate news. A 2025 Review of Quantitative Finance and Accounting paper builds a flow measure from OI/volume changes with predictive power, but cites the caveat that traders "may not be able to distinguish between informed and non-informed trades" — [Augustin et al.](https://publikationen.ub.uni-frankfurt.de/frontdoor/index/index/year/2016/docId/41680); [Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11156-025-01427-z)
- Bergsma et al. (2020): moneyness-weighted option volume predicts next-day stock returns (secondary summary) — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/?p=55387)

### Inferences
- Academic predictability results are measured before costs and often use signed or non-public data (e.g., open/close by trader type). A retail trader acting on a public alert *after* the print, by buying short-dated OTM options at 10-30% spreads, would likely give up any edge to execution costs. This is an inference; it has not been tested.
- A transparency-focused flow tool could add value by showing the *forward performance of its own alerts net of realistic spread* and labelling flow that is likely hedging or spread legs vs. directional.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or regulator study on the profitability of following retail flow-alert services (Unusual Whales, Cheddar Flow, etc.).
- No US causal evidence that gamification features increase *options* losses specifically. Massachusetts made no finding of behavioural harm.

## Q5. Which educational or transparency interventions have measurable effects on retail outcomes?

### Takeaway
Rigorous causal evidence is thin. The strongest US evidence is indirect: costs are the main loss driver and are invisible to traders (BPS), and limit-order users and sellers do far better. The best-documented interventions are outside the US (Korea's mandatory education, mock trading and deposits). Those reduced speculative activity but reportedly pushed it into other high-risk venues, and I found no measured effect on losses.

### Cited Findings
- Korea's 2011 domestic derivatives reform (higher deposit plus mandatory education) "eased excessive speculative demand" but sharply reduced liquidity and created a "balloon effect" into overseas products and crypto (official commentary, not a study) — [Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/45010/koreas-derivatives-market-at-30-trading-shifts-to-overseas-and-crypto-markets)
- Korea from Dec 15, 2025: at least 1 hour of education and 3 hours of simulated trading before overseas derivatives. Prompted by retail losses of ₩457.4B (2022), ₩445.8B (2023), ₩360.9B (2024) and ₩373.7B (Jan-Oct 2025). Single-stock leveraged ETFs from Aug 19, 2026 need 5 days of mock trading, 3 hours of education and a ₩30M deposit; experts doubt the training gives meaningful protection — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/regulation/fx-trading-rules-tighten-as-south-korea-targets-retail-investors-risk/); [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/economy/20260423/korea-preps-single-stock-leveraged-etfs-as-experts-question-investor-safeguards)
- Disclosure comprehension: an experiment on SEC Form CRS found disclosure significantly improved comprehension of account types. It measures comprehension, not trading outcomes — [arXiv 2206.00117](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00117)
- Bogousslavsky-Muravyev: retail traders who probably use limit orders lose only about 0.9% per option trade despite 5-10% spreads, and multi-day traders lose about 0.9%. This suggests execution behaviour, not just product choice, drives outcomes (cross-sectional, not causal) — [PDF](https://www.lsu.edu/business/files/event-files/2025-finance-mardi-gras/retail_option_trading_v2.pdf)
- BPS: spread costs "are not as transparent as brokerage fees and are likely to be overlooked by retail investors." BPS also cite evidence that investors "learn worse after experiencing financial losses" — [JF 2023](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Retail%20Trading%20in%20Options%20and%20the%20Rise%20of%20the%20Big%20Three%20Wholesalers.pdf)
- Ernst & Spatt recommend expanded options data disclosure as a policy fix — [CMU Tepper](https://www.cmu.edu/tepper/news/stories/high-option-trading-fees-barriers-competition)

### Inferences
- The interventions most consistent with the evidence are **cost salience** (showing realised spread paid per trade and in aggregate), **order-type nudges** (limit at or near mid rather than market orders on wide-spread contracts), **IV-vs-realised feedback** around events, and **honest P&L attribution** (buy vs. sell, DTE bucket, held-through-event). These target the measured loss drivers. Their causal effect on US options traders is untested.
- Gating approaches (education requirements, deposits) appear to reduce participation more than they improve outcomes, and may push activity to less regulated venues (Korean experience).

### Gaps
- No RCT or natural experiment found measuring how cost or fill-quality disclosure affects US retail options P&L.
- ESMA's CFD "% of retail accounts lose money" mandatory warning (an obvious analogue) was not evaluated in the sources found. I found no study of its effect on outcomes.
- No study of Robinhood's 2021 removal of confetti or other UI changes on options trading behaviour.
